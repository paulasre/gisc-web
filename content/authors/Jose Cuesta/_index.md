José A. Cuesta graduated in physics at Universidad Complutense de Madrid in 1987 and got his PhD in physics at the same university in 1992. That year he joined the Department of Mathematics of Universidad Carlos III de Madrid, where he became an associate professor in 1995 and a full professor in 2011. He is a member of the Institute for Biocomputation and Physics of Complex Systems (BIFI) since March 2012; an Associate researcher of York Centre for Complex System Analysis (YCCSA), at The University of York, since September 2013; and a member of UC3M-BS Institute of Financial Big Data (IFiBiD), since September 2015. His Erdős number is 3.

For a long period—including his PhD—he applied density functional theory to the study of phases and phase transitions in complex fluids (liquid crystals, mixtures, colloids, etc.). When he moved to Universidad Carlos III he worked for a short interval in semiconductor theory. But his current research interests lie in the theory of complex systems, both biologically and socially motivated, with special emphasis on evolutionary dynamics and microbial ecology.
